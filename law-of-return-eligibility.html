Law of Return eligibility: who can make aliyah?

A plain-language summary of who the Law of Return covers, and who it does not.

The core rule

Israel's Law of Return, 1950 states that every Jew has the right to immigrate to Israel. Immigration is by an immigrant visa (or, for someone already in Israel, an immigrant certificate).

Who counts as a Jew under the law

For the purposes of the Law of Return, a Jew is a person born to a Jewish mother, or who has converted to Judaism, and who is not a member of another religion.

Family members: the three-generation and spouse rules

A 1970 amendment (section 4A) extends the same rights to:

  • the child and grandchild of a Jew,
  • the spouse of a Jew,
  • the spouse of a child or grandchild of a Jew,
  • and the widow or widower of a Jew, or of a child or grandchild of a Jew.

This is why a person whose only Jewish parent is their father, or who has one Jewish grandparent, may be eligible. It does not matter whether the Jewish relative is still alive or ever immigrated to Israel. The family rights do not apply to a person who was Jewish and voluntarily changed their religion.

Exceptions

The Minister of the Interior may refuse an immigrant visa if the applicant acts against the Jewish people, may endanger public health or state security, or has a criminal past that may endanger public welfare.

Examples

  • Only my father is Jewish. You may be eligible as the child of a Jew.
  • One of my grandparents was Jewish. You may be eligible as the grandchild of a Jew, and your spouse may be eligible too.
  • I converted. Conversion documents are part of the proof the Jewish Agency asks for; additional requirements may apply, so ask your aliyah representative.

Eligibility under the Law of Return is a civil matter. It is not the same as Jewish status for religious purposes in Israel, such as marriage through the Rabbinate.

How eligibility is decided

Eligibility is assessed person by person in the official process, by the Jewish Agency and the Israeli authorities. Get Out Now does not decide eligibility.
