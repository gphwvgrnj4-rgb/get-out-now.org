Emergency aliyah: how it works today

What happens when Jews need to make aliyah quickly, what has worked before, and what is still missing.

In immediate danger? Contact local emergency services or the nearest Israeli embassy or consulate first.

Is there a separate "emergency aliyah" visa?

No. Emergency aliyah is not a separate legal category. The same eligibility rules of Israel's Law of Return, 1950 apply in a crisis as in ordinary times. What changes in an emergency is the speed and the logistics: where people are met, how quickly their eligibility is confirmed and how they travel. See our guide to Law of Return eligibility.

What has happened in past emergencies

When war broke out in Ukraine in February 2022, the Jewish Agency announced six aliyah processing stations at Ukraine's borders with Poland, Moldova, Romania and Hungary, operated with Nativ and Israel's Ministry of Foreign Affairs. At its Warsaw immigration center, a portable Israeli consulate helped people obtain aliyah visas under expedited procedures, while people were housed in hotels until their flights.

That response shows that fast processing is possible. It also shows the gap: in a crisis, institutions must first find people, then check documents, often at a border or in a hotel lobby.

What you can do now, before any crisis

  • Know whether you are likely eligible. The Law of Return covers Jews and their children, grandchildren and spouses, with some exceptions.
  • Gather your documents early. The Jewish Agency says the process usually takes 3 to 6 months, depending largely on how quickly applicants can supply the requested documents. See documents to gather now.
  • Know who handles your file. It depends on where you live: see aliyah from France, the UK, the US and Canada.
  • Use official channels. The Jewish Agency runs a Global Service Center and an online aliyah questionnaire. North American applicants apply through Nefesh B'Nefesh.

What is still missing

Today nobody can say, in advance, how many people are entitled to come home under the Law of Return and have not yet moved, or where they are. Our petition asks the Jewish Agency and Nefesh B'Nefesh to establish a national registry: a pre-verification screener where one core document leads to a clear eligibility answer and a dated credential, before a crisis rather than during one.

This is a proposed structure. No such arrangement exists today, and nothing here implies endorsement by, or an agreement with, the Jewish Agency, Nefesh B'Nefesh or any Israeli authority. Read how the proposed screener would work.

Need help understanding your options?

Our volunteers reply, free of charge, to people who want to understand their options under the Law of Return. Ask for help. We will never ask for money or passport scans over chat.

This guide is general information, not legal advice. Eligibility and procedures are decided only by the official process; always confirm current requirements with the Jewish Agency or Nefesh B'Nefesh.
